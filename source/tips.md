# Tips — fixed reminder + rotating tip

Read-only reference. `prep` picks the next tip number and puts it in
`working/session-brief.md`; the Project opens the voice session with the fixed
reminder below plus that one tip. Keep tips short — one sentence, said once, not a lecture.

---

## Fixed voice reminder (every session, no exceptions)

> Speak at real B2 speed with idioms and contractions — don't slow down or simplify for me.
> Let me finish before you correct anything. Don't be soft — no empty praise. Cap corrections
> at 3-6, given at the end. If I say "just tell me," answer straight and note that I used it.

## Rotating tips

Cycle through in order, one per session. Wrap back to 1 after 12. `prep` tracks the
current number in `notes.md` so it doesn't repeat.

1. **Push past the first answer.** If your reply is short and safe, that's the signal to add "why" or an example — not to stop.
2. **He/she is not automatic for you.** Persian «او» has no gender. Slow down on this pronoun specifically until it's reflexive.
3. **Reach for a chunk, not a word.** Before answering, ask: is there a ready-made phrase for this, not just a vocabulary word?
4. **Articles are chunks too.** "a/an/the" don't map from Persian — treat each noun phrase as something to memorize, not derive.
5. **Silence is fine. Persian is not.** If you're stuck, pause in English rather than switching languages mid-thought.
6. **Self-correct before I do.** If a sentence sounds wrong as it leaves your mouth, try fixing it yourself before I step in.
7. **Longer sentences risk verb drift.** Persian is SOV — in a long sentence, check the verb didn't wander toward the end.
8. **"Just tell me" is not a failure.** Use it when you're actually stuck, not as a shortcut around effort.
9. **Present perfect is a real B1 ceiling.** If a sentence about the past feels awkward, check: simple past or present perfect?
10. **Idioms are for stealing, not memorizing in bulk.** One used correctly beats five recognized passively.
11. **Numbers plus nouns need the plural.** "five books," not "five book" — this one is easy to fix once you notice it.
12. **Fluency is speed with fewer restarts, not perfect grammar.** In the 4/3/2 drill, keep going through a mistake — don't stop to fix it mid-round.
