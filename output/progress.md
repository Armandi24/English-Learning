# Progress — the numbers

Fluency feels like nothing while it is happening. That is normal, it is not a signal,
and it is the single most common reason people quit at the B1 plateau.

**This file is the antidote.** Feelings about progress are unreliable; these numbers are not.

---

## The goal

> Hold a real conversation — a client meeting, a job interview, a debate, explaining a
> design concept or a cosmology idea — **without hunting for words.** Speak at length,
> get pushed back on, and keep going, in English, without the sentence falling apart.

**B1 → B2.** Cambridge estimates 180-260 guided hours. At 45 min/day, ~6 days a week:
roughly **7-9 months**. Consistency is the variable that matters. Intensity is not.

---

## 4/3/2 fluency drill — words per minute

The most sensitive short-term measure. In the original study, WPM rose **~23 words**
from round 1 to round 3 within a single session, and hesitations dropped sharply.

Two things to watch:
- **Within a session:** R1 → R3 should rise. If it doesn't, the topic was too hard or too unfamiliar.
- **Across weeks:** the R1 number should climb. That's real fluency gain, not warm-up effect.
